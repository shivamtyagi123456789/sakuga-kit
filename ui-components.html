    Smoothness & Performance Pass — what changed technically

    • One spring system: every animation now resolves to four shared curves — --sp-glide (reveals, indicators), --sp-pop (pops, stamps, badges), --sp-snap (digits, toggles), --sp-soft (hovers, sheens) — with three shared durations. Motion feels related across all 169 components instead of each card inventing its own timing.
    • Off-screen pause: an IntersectionObserver (140px early-warning margin) tags every card data-off when it leaves the viewport; all CSS animation inside a tagged card pauses, and the heavy JS loops — liquid ring canvas, marquee, ink bubble spring, bunk needle physics — skip their work while hidden or off-screen. The live feed and equalizers also sleep when the tab is hidden.
    • GPU-friendly motion: movement is transform + opacity wherever possible. The gradient mesh no longer animates a hue-rotate filter every frame (that repainted constantly) — its colour drift is now an opacity crossfade between static gradients, and its blur dropped 46px → 38px with a composited layer.
    • Pointer effects throttled: magnetic buttons and the card tilt/light-sweep now run through requestAnimationFrame — at most one update per frame, no layout thrash on fast mouse moves.
    • Richer, but cheaper, backgrounds: the page gained layered static radial washes (amber top-right, teal mid-left — painted once), a fixed ambient light field that drifts on transform only, deeper static layering on the deadline card, drawn gold rules under Wave heads, and still (non-animated) selection halos on chips. More depth, fewer per-frame repaints.
    • Weakest lifted a notch: Cards gained hover lift + sheen sweep; Chips gained the selection halo — a second simultaneous layer each, without touching content, variants or palette identity.
    • Reduced motion respected: under prefers-reduced-motion, ambient animation stops, durations collapse, smooth-scroll turns off, and click bursts reduce to a single calm ring.

The physics pieces (Waves 1–2, Toggles, Lists) still lead; Cards (60) and Chips (62) remain the honest weak spots.
